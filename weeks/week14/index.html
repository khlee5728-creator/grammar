to ..., for ..., and so that ... Unit 62

목적을 나타내는 to... / so that

A to + verb for purpose

Subject + verb ... + to + verb (= in order to / so as to)

We use to + verb to say WHY somebody does something. 📖 Unit 62A

"I called the restaurant to make a reservation." — purpose
"The president has bodyguards to protect him." — purpose

D so that

Use so that (not to ...) especially with can/could and will/would.

"She's learning English so that she can study in Canada." — so that + can/could

for + noun / for + -ing vs. to + verb (1) Unit 62

for + 명사 / for + -ing vs. to + 동사

B for + noun

C for + -ing vs to + verb

for + noun / -ing  /  to + verb

for + -ing = purpose of a thing  |  to + verb = person's purpose

for + noun

"We stopped for gas."

to + verb

"We stopped to get gas."

📖 GIU Unit 62, p.124

for + -ing vs. to + verb (2) Unit 62

for + -ing (사물의 용도) vs. to + verb (사람의 목적)

C for + -ing vs to + verb

"This brush is for washing the dishes." — for + -ing = purpose of the brush
"I went into the kitchen to wash the dishes." — to + verb = why the person did it
"I went into the kitchen for washing the dishes."

Adjective + to ... Unit 63

형용사 + to부정사 (hard to understand / easy to make / interesting to talk to ...)

A difficult to understand, easy to make, etc.

It's + adjective + to + verb  ↔  Subject + is + adjective + to + verb

"It is hard to understand him." = "He is hard to understand."

"He is hard to understand." — = It is hard to understand him.
"Is this water safe to drink?" — = Is it safe to drink this water?
"Nicole is interesting to talk to." — Note "to" at the end.

to ... and Preposition + -ing (interested / sorry) Unit 64

interested in vs. interested to / sorry for vs. sorry to

B interested in doing vs interested to hear

interested in -ing = thinking of doing it  |  interested to hear = it was interesting for me

"I was interested to hear that Tanya left her job." — = it was interesting for me
"Are you interested in joining the club?" — = thinking of joining

C sorry to do vs sorry for doing

"I'm sorry for shouting at you yesterday." — sorry for -ing = apologize

see somebody do and see somebody doing Unit 65

지각동사: see/hear/watch + 원형/동명사

A see someone do = complete action

see/hear/watch/feel + object + base form — complete action
see/hear/watch/feel + object + -ing — action in progress

see somebody doing (in progress) Unit 65

지각동사: -ing (진행 중인 행동)

B see someone doing = in progress

+ base form (complete)

"I saw him do something." — complete action

+ -ing (in progress)

"I saw him doing something." — in the middle of it

C hear/watch/notice etc.

"I saw Tom walking along the street."
"I could hear it raining." — -ing = action in progress when perceived

-ing Phrases (He hurt his knee playing football.) Unit 66

-ing 분사구문 — 동시 동작 / 원인 (= while ... / because ...)

A -ing phrases: simultaneous actions

Subject + verb ... + -ing — simultaneous / = while ... was -ing

Two things happening at the same time / during another action

"A man ran out of the house shouting." — simultaneous actions
"Joe hurt his knee playing football." — = while playing
"Be careful crossing the road." — = when you cross

📖 GIU Unit 66, p.132

-ing Phrases — Having done / Feeling tired Unit 66

Having done... (먼저 완료된 행동)

B Having done...

Having + past participle ... — earlier action

Use having + p.p. when one action happens before another.

"Having found a hotel, we looked for somewhere to eat."
"Having finished her work, she went home."

C Feeling tired, I went to bed early

-ing / Not -ing ... — reason / cause (= because ...)
"Feeling tired, I went to bed early." — = because I felt tired
"Not knowing what to do, I called my friend." — = because I didn't know

📖 GIU Unit 66, p.132

Common Mistakes for Korean Speakers

한국어 화자가 자주 하는 실수

⚠ L1 Interference: Perception verbs do/doing distinction

Korean does not distinguish "see someone do" and "see someone doing".

"I saw him cross the road." — complete action
"I saw him crossing the road." — in progress
⚠ L1 Interference: Participle clauses feel unnatural

Korean uses connective endings (-면서, -고, -아서); English uses -ing phrases.

"Walking along the street, ..." — = 걸으면서
"Not knowing what to do, ..." — = 몰라서
