to ..., for ..., and so that ... Unit 62

목적을 나타내는 to... / so that

A to + verb for purpose

Subject + verb ... + to + verb (= in order to / so as to)

We use to + verb to say WHY somebody does something. 📖 Unit 62A

"I called the restaurant to make a reservation." — purpose
"The president has bodyguards to protect him." — purpose

D so that

Use so that (not to ...) especially with can/could and will/would.

"She's learning English so that she can study in Canada." — so that + can/could

📖 GIU Unit 62, p.124

noun + to ... and for + noun Unit 62B-C

a place to park / money to buy / for + 명사 vs. to + 동사

B noun + to-infinitive

a place to park, something to eat, work to do  |  also money / time / chance / opportunity + to do

"It's hard to find a place to park downtown."
"They gave us money to buy food." — money + to + verb

▸ Preposition stays at the end (sit on / talk to / write with)

"Is there a chair to sit on?" / "I get lonely if there's nobody to talk to."

C for + noun vs. to + verb

for + noun

"We stopped for gas."
"I had to run for the bus."

to + verb

"We stopped to get gas."
"I had to run to catch the bus."

📖 GIU Unit 62, p.124

for + -ing vs. to + verb & What ... for? Unit 62C

사물의 용도 vs 사람의 목적 & for + sb + to do & What ... for?

C for + -ing vs to + verb

"This brush is for washing the dishes." — for + -ing = purpose of the brush
"I went into the kitchen to wash the dishes." — to + verb = why the person did it
"I went into the kitchen for washing the dishes."

▸ for + somebody + to do something

"There weren't any chairs for us to sit on, so we sat on the floor."

▸ What ... for? — ask about purpose

"What is this switch for?" — = what is it used for?
"What did you do that for?" — = why did you do that?

📖 GIU Unit 62, p.124

Adjective + to ... — basic pattern Unit 63A

형용사 + to부정사 — 기본 변환 공식 (It's hard to understand him ↔ He is hard to understand)

A hard to understand, safe to drink, etc.

It's + adjective + to + verb + object  ↔  Subject + is + adjective + to + verb

Transformation: the object of the infinitive moves to the subject position; to + verb stays.

Adjective list: easy / nice / cheap / good / safe / exciting / interesting / difficult / dangerous / expensive / impossible

"It is hard to understand him." = "He is hard to understand." — object him → subject He
"Is this water safe to drink?" = "Is it safe to drink this water?"
"The exam questions were very hard. They were impossible to answer."

📖 GIU Unit 63A, p.126

Adjective + to ... — extensions Unit 63A

전치사 끝 (interesting to talk to)  ·  형용사+명사+to do (difficult question to answer)

A Two important extensions of the §A pattern

▸ Preposition stays at the end (talk to / answer __ / sit on)

"Nicole is interesting to talk to." — not interesting to talk to her
"This pen is easy to write with." — verb requires "write WITH"

▸ Also adjective + noun + to do — (a) noun-modifier form

Pattern: a / an + adjective + noun + to + verb

"This is a difficult question to answer." — not to answer it (object already inside)
"It's a great place to live."

📖 GIU Unit 63A, p.126

nice of you to ... / sorry to ... Unit 63B-C

사람 평가 (kind/generous of you ...)  ·  감정 반응 (happy/glad/sorry to ...)

B It's nice of (somebody) to ... — evaluating somebody's action

Adjective list: nice / kind / generous / careless / stupid / inconsiderate / unfair / typical

"It was nice of you to take me to the airport."
"It's generous of Abby to let us stay in her apartment."

C sorry to ... / surprised to ... — reacting to something

Adjective list: sorry / happy / glad / pleased / relieved / surprised / amazed / sad / disappointed

"I'm sorry to hear that your mother isn't well."
"Was Julia surprised to see you?"

📖 GIU Unit 63B-C, p.126

the first to ... & sure / likely / bound to ... Unit 63D-E

서수·한정사 + to ... (the first / only / last)  ·  가능성 (sure / bound / likely to ...)

D the next / the last / the only / the first / the second + to ...

"The next plane to leave was at Gate 4."
"Everybody was late except me. I was the only one to arrive on time."
"If I have any more news, you will be the first to know." (= the first person to know)

E sure / likely / bound + to happen

sure to / bound to = certain to happen (100%)  |  likely to = probable (high chance, not certain)

📖 "She's bound to pass the exam (= she is sure to pass)." (Unit 63E)

"Carla is a very good student. She's bound to pass the exam." (= sure to pass)
"It's possible I'll win the lottery, but it's not likely to happen." (= not probable)

📖 GIU Unit 63D-E, p.126

to ... (afraid to do) and Preposition + -ing (afraid of -ing) Unit 64

afraid to do vs. afraid of -ing

A afraid to do vs afraid of doing

① afraid to do = don't want to (it's dangerous / choose not to)

② afraid of -ing = worried something bad might happen

📖 "I am afraid of something happening = it might happen and I don't want it." (Unit 64A)

① afraid to do

"People are afraid to walk here at night." (too dangerous)

② afraid of -ing

"We were afraid of falling." (might happen)

📖 GIU Unit 64, p.128

to ... and Preposition + -ing (interested / sorry) Unit 64

관심 (interested in -ing vs interested to + base)  ·  유감 (sorry for vs sorry to)

B ① interested in + -ing (thinking of doing) vs ② interested to hear (it was interesting)

"Are you interested in joining the club?" (= thinking of joining)
"I was interested to hear that Tanya left her job." (= it was interesting)

C sorry for + -ing  vs  sorry to + verb (3 uses: past / reaction / at the moment)

① "I'm sorry for shouting at you yesterday." = "I'm sorry I shouted at you yesterday." (past apology)
② "I'm sorry to hear that Nicky lost her job." (reaction to news — not sorry for hearing)
③ "I'm sorry to bother you, but I need to ask a question." (at this moment)

D Common pairs — related verbs, different complement

want + to do
think of + -ing
hope + to do
dream of + -ing
fail + to do
succeed in + -ing
plan + to do
look forward to + -ing
promise + to do
insist on + -ing

📖 GIU Unit 64, p.128

see somebody do — complete action Unit 65A

지각동사 (see/hear/listen to/watch/feel) + 원형 — 전체 동작을 보거나 들었을 때

A see + object + base form = complete action

📖 "Somebody did something — I saw this = I saw + somebody + do something." (Unit 65A)

see / hear / listen to / watch / feel + object + base form — saw the whole action
"I saw Tom get into his car and drive away." — complete action
"I didn't hear you come in." — You came in — I didn't hear it.
"Julia suddenly felt somebody touch her on the shoulder." — felt + base form

📖 GIU Unit 65, p.130

see somebody doing (in progress) Unit 65B-C

지각동사 (see/hear/feel) + -ing (진행 중)  ·  비교: 원형(완료) vs -ing(진행)

B see + object + -ing = action in progress

see / hear / listen to / watch / feel / smell / find + object + -ing
"I could hear it raining." — it was raining — I could hear it
"Can you smell something burning?" — smell + -ing
"We found him sitting under a tree eating an apple." — found + -ing

C Compare: ① see + base form (complete) vs ② see + -ing (in progress)

① base form = complete

"I saw him jump over the wall and run away."

② -ing = in progress

"I saw him walking along the street."

▸ Sometimes the difference is not important: seen her dance OR dancing are both OK.

📖 GIU Unit 65, p.130

-ing Phrases (He hurt his knee playing football.) Unit 66

-ing 분사구문 — 동시 동작 / 원인 (= while ... / because ...)

A -ing phrases: simultaneous / during another action

📖 "You can use -ing ... when two things happen at the same time." (Unit 66A)

Subject + verb ... + -ing  =  while / when + Subject + was -ing
"Kate is in the kitchen making coffee." — at the same time
"A man ran out of the house shouting." — simultaneous actions
"Joe hurt his knee playing football." — = while he was playing

▸ Also while doing / when doing: "Joe hurt his knee while playing." / "Be careful when crossing the street."

📖 GIU Unit 66, p.132

-ing Phrases — Having done / Feeling tired Unit 66

Having done... (먼저 완료된 행동)

B Having done... / After -ing (earlier action)

Having + past participle ...  or  After + -ing ...
"Having finished her work, she went home."
"After finishing her work, she went home." — after + -ing = same meaning

C -ing for reason / cause (= because ...)

📖 Register note: "These structures are used more in written English than in spoken English." (Unit 66C) — formal/literary register

"Feeling tired, I went to bed early." — = because I felt tired

▸ Variants — Being + adj. / Not + -ing / Having + p.p.

"Being unemployed, he doesn't have much money." — Being + adjective (state)
"Not having a car, he finds it difficult to get around." — Not + -ing (negative)
"Having seen the movie twice, I didn't want to see it again." — Having + p.p. (anterior state)

📖 GIU Unit 66, p.132

Common Mistakes for Korean Speakers

한국어 화자가 자주 하는 실수

⚠ L1 Interference: Perception verbs do/doing distinction

Korean does not distinguish "see someone do" and "see someone doing".

"I saw him cross the road." — complete action
"I saw him crossing the road." — in progress
⚠ L1 Interference: Participle clauses feel unnatural

Korean uses connective endings (-면서, -고, -아서); English uses -ing phrases.

"Walking along the street, ..." — = while walking
"Not knowing what to do, ..." — = because I didn't know
