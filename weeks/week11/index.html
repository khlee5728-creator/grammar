Reported Speech 1 — Direct vs Reported Unit 45A

간접 화법 기본 — 직접 화법과 간접 화법 두 가지 표현 방법

📖 GIU Unit 45A, p.90

A Two ways to tell what somebody said: direct or reported speech

When you want to tell somebody what someone said, you can use either direct speech or reported speech. 📖 Unit 45A

Direct speech: Dan said, "I am feeling sick."
Reported speech: Dan said that he was feeling sick.
Compare: direct Dan said, "I am feeling sick." vs reported Dan said that he was feeling sick.

Reported Speech 1 — Tense Backshift Unit 45B

간접 화법의 시제 후퇴 — 동사 형태 변환

📖 GIU Unit 45B, p.90

When reporting what someone said, the main verb is usually past, so the verb in the original direct speech also shifts back. You can leave out that. 📖 Unit 45B

Reported Speech 1 — past simple stays or → past perfect Unit 45C

간접화법에서 단순 과거는 그대로 두거나 과거완료로 바꿀 수 있음

C The simple past can stay the same OR change to the past perfect

The simple past (did/saw/knew) can either stay the same in reported speech or change to the past perfect (had done / had seen / had known). 📖 Unit 45C

Direct: "I woke up feeling sick, so I didn't go to work."
Reported (past simple — stays): Dan said (that) he woke up feeling sick, so he didn't go to work.
Reported (past perfect — also OK): Dan said (that) he had woken up feeling sick, so he hadn't gone to work.

📖 GIU Unit 45C, p.90

Reported Speech 2 — Don't always change verb Unit 46A

상황이 여전히 같으면 동사를 과거로 바꿀 필요 없음

📖 GIU Unit 46A, p.92

A If the situation is still the same, no need to change verb to past

We do not always change the verb in reported speech. If the situation is still the same, no need to shift back. 📖 Unit 46A

Direct: John said, "My new job is boring." → Reported: John said that his new job is boring. (still boring now)
Direct: Olivia said, "I want to go to South America next year." → Reported: Olivia told me that she wants to go to South America next year. (still wants to go)
But if situation changed/finished: John said that his new job was boring. (no longer boring → past tense)

Reported Speech 2 — When reality differs Unit 46B

말한 내용이 현재 사실과 다를 때는 반드시 과거형

📖 GIU Unit 46B, p.92

B Change verb when what was said differs from reality

You need to change the verb to past in reported speech when what was said is different from what is really true now. 📖 Unit 46B

Earlier (Lauren said): "Joe is in the hospital."
Later (you see Joe on the street): "Lauren said you were in the hospital." (NOT "you are in the hospital" — no longer true)
Key point: Joe is no longer in the hospital, so the present tense cannot be used. Use past tense when reality differs from what was said.

Reported Speech 2 — say and tell Unit 46

say와 tell의 구분 / 간접 명령문

C Say vs tell

say vs. tell

With a person → tell: Emma told me that you were sick.

Without a person → say: Emma said that you were sick.

D Tell/ask someone to do

tell / ask somebody to...

tell + somebody + (not) to...
"Drink water." → The doctor told me to drink water.
"Don't work too hard." → I told Joe not to work too hard.
"Can you help me?" → Jackie asked me to help her.

📖 GIU Unit 46, p.92–93

Questions 1 — Subject inversion & do/does/did Unit 47A-B

의문문 어순 기본 — subject 도치 + 단순 시제의 do/does/did

📖 GIU Unit 47A-B, p.94

A Subject comes AFTER the first verb (inversion)

In questions, subject comes after the first verb: subject + verb → verb + subject. 📖 Unit 47A

Will Tom be here tomorrow? / Have you been working hard? / When was the house built? / Is Katherine working today? (NOT "Is working Katherine")

B Use do/does/did for simple present/past — BUT not when who/what is subject

Simple present: do/does. Simple past: did. But: don't use do/does/did when who/what is the subject.

Do you live nearby? / What time does the movie start? / Why did the train stop?
who = object (use do/did): "Emma called somebody." → Who did Emma call?
who = subject (no do/did): "Somebody called Emma." → Who called Emma? / What happened to you? (NOT "What did happen")

Questions 1 — Preposition at end of question Unit 47C

전치사 끝 위치 — 의문사로 시작하는 의문문

📖 GIU Unit 47C, p.94

C Prepositions (in, for, etc.) usually go at the end of the question

In questions beginning with who/what/where, prepositions go at the end of the question. 📖 Unit 47C

"Where are you from?"
"What was the weather like?"
"Who do you want to speak to?"
"Which job has Lauren applied for?"
Formal style (preposition + whom): "To whom do you wish to speak?"

Questions 1 — Negative questions (isn't it ...? / didn't you ...?) Unit 47D

부정 의문문 — 놀라움 / 동의 기대 / 제안

📖 GIU Unit 47D, p.94

D Negative questions express surprise, expecting agreement, or suggestions

Negative questions express surprise / expecting agreement / "Why don't...?" suggestion. 📖 Unit 47D

Surprise: "Didn't you hear the doorbell? I rang it three times."
Surprise: "Haven't we met before?"
Yes/No answer caution: "Don't you want to go?" — "Yes." (= I want to go) / "No." (= I don't want to go)
Why don't...? suggestion: "Why don't we eat out tonight?" (NOT "Why we don't eat")

Questions 2 — Embedded questions (Do you know where...?) Unit 48A

내포 의문문 — statement 어순 + do/does/did 제거 + if/whether

📖 GIU Unit 48A, p.96

A Embedded questions: statement word order, drop do/does/did

When a question is part of a longer sentence (Do you know...? / I don't know... / Could you tell me...?), the word order changes to statement form and do/does/did is dropped. 📖 Unit 48A

Direct: "Where has Tom gone?" → Embedded: Do you know where Tom has gone? (NOT "has Tom gone")
Direct: "What time does the movie start?" → Embedded: Do you know what time the movie starts? (drop does)
Direct: "Why did she leave early?" → Embedded: I wonder why she left early. (drop did)
Yes/No question → if or whether: "Did anybody see you?" → I don't know if anybody saw me. (or whether anybody saw me)

Questions 2 — Reported questions (He asked me where...) Unit 48B

간접 화법 의문문 — Unit 48A 어순 규칙 + 과거형 시제 (Unit 45 backshift)

📖 GIU Unit 48B, p.96

B Reported questions: same word order changes + past tense (backshift)

Reported questions also follow Unit 48A's word order rule, and the main verb backshifts to past tense (see Unit 45). 📖 Unit 48B

Direct: "Where are you going?" → Reported: The police officer asked us where we were going.
Direct: "What time does the store close?" → Reported: Olivia wanted to know what time the store closed. (drop does + past tense)
Interview example 1: "Why did you apply?" → She asked why I had applied.
Interview example 2: "How long have you been working?" → She asked how long I had been working.
Yes/No question → if/whether: "Are you willing to travel?" → She asked if (or whether) I was willing to travel.

Auxiliary Verbs — basics & interest reactions Unit 49A-B

보조동사: 반복 회피·부정 응답 / He has? — 놀라움·관심 표현

📖 GIU Unit 49A-B, p.98

A Auxiliary to avoid repeating + deny statements

Use an auxiliary (have/do/can/was/will, etc.) to avoid repeating the main verb, or to deny what somebody says. 📖 Unit 49A

"Have you locked the door?" "Yes, I have." (= I have locked the door)
"Mike wasn't working, but Emily was." (= Emily was working)
"You'll be the next president." "No, I won't." (denying the statement)

B He has? / She isn't? / You are? — show interest or surprise

Use He has? / She isn't? / You are?, etc. to show interest or surprise. 📖 Unit 49B

"Josh has changed a lot." — "He has? I haven't seen him lately."
"I'm getting married." — "You are? Really?"

so do I / neither do I & I think so Unit 49C-D

so / neither + I think so 표현

📖 GIU Unit 49C-D, p.98

C so and neither — agreement (verb before subject)

so + auxiliary + subject (= me too) / neither + auxiliary + subject (= me neither). 📖 Unit 49C (verb before subject)

so (긍정 동의): "I'm tired." — "So am I." (= I'm tired too)
neither (부정 동의): "I never read newspapers." — "Neither do I." (= I don't read newspapers either)
"I passed the exam, and so did Paul." (NOT "so Paul did")

D I think so / I hope so / I suppose so — avoid repeating clause

Negative forms: I don't think so | I hope not | I'm afraid not | I guess not | I suppose not

"Are those people Korean?" — "I think so." (= I think they are Korean)
"Do you think it will rain?" — "I hope not." (NOT "I don't hope so")

Tag Questions — Basics (Have you? / Wasn't it?) Unit 50A

부가 의문문 — 기본 사용 + do/does/did

📖 GIU Unit 50A, p.100

A Tag questions = mini-questions at the end of a sentence

Have you? / Wasn't it?, etc. are tag questions. Use auxiliary (have/was/will, etc.). For simple present/past, use do/does/did. 📖 Unit 50A

"Emily plays the piano, doesn't she?" "Well, yes, but not very well."
"You didn't lock the door, did you?" "No, I forgot."

Tag Questions — Affirmative/Negative rules Unit 50B

긍정/부정 태그 규칙 + Yes/No 응답 의미

📖 GIU Unit 50B, p.100

B Affirmative + negative tag / Negative + affirmative tag

Affirmative sentence + negative tag (usually) / Negative sentence + affirmative tag

Affirmative + negative tag: "Kate will be here soon, won't she?"
Affirmative + negative tag: "There was a lot of traffic, wasn't there?"
Negative + affirmative tag: "Kate won't be late, will she?"
Negative + affirmative tag: "You haven't eaten yet, have you?"
⚠️ Yes/No answer meaning: "You're not going out, are you?" — "Yes." (= Yes, I am going out) / "No." (= No, I am not going out)

Tag Questions — Intonation meaning Unit 50C

억양에 따른 의미 — 동의 기대 vs 진짜 질문 vs 부탁

📖 GIU Unit 50C, p.100

C Meaning depends on intonation — voice down (agreement) or up (real question)

Meaning depends on intonation: voice ↓ = expecting agreement (not a real question), voice ↑ = real question. 📖 Unit 50C

↓ (expecting agreement): "It's a nice day, isn't it ↘?" "Yes, beautiful."
↓ (expecting agreement): "David doesn't look well, does he ↘?"
↑ (real question): "You haven't seen Kate today, have you ↗?" (= Have you seen Kate today?)
Neg + aff tag ↑ = asking for a favor: "You couldn't do me a favor, could you ↗?"

Tag Questions — Special tag forms Unit 50D

특수 태그 형태 — imperative, I'm... + 추가 학습

📖 GIU Unit 50D, p.100

D Special tag forms: imperative → will you? / I'm → aren't I?

After imperative: "Listen to me, will you?"
After "I'm...": "I'm right, aren't I?" (NOT "am I not?")

Supplementary — additional tag forms (not in textbook Unit 50)

After "Let's...": "Let's go, shall we?"
Negative words (nobody/nothing) → affirmative tag: "Nobody called, did they?"

Direct vs. Reported Speech Units 45–46

직접 화법 vs. 간접 화법

Direct Speech

Dan said, "I'm feeling sick."

Anna said, "I'll call you when I get back."

Uses quotation marks, exact words, original tenses

Reported Speech

Dan said he was feeling sick.

Anna said she would call me when she got back.

No quotation marks, tense backshift, pronoun changes

Common Mistakes for Korean Speakers

한국어 화자의 흔한 실수

L1 Interference: No Tense Backshift
"He said he is tired." → "He said he was tired."
Backshift: "is" → "was" 시제 후퇴: "is" → "was"
L1 Interference: Question Word Order
"He asked where did I live." → "He asked where I lived."

Week 11 Summary

11주차 요약

  • Tense backshift: present→past, past→past perfect, will→would, can→could
  • Say vs. tell: tell + somebody; say (no person); tell/ask somebody to...
  • Reported questions: statement word order, no "do/did"; embedded: Do you know where...?
  • Who/what subject: Who called Emma? (no did) vs. Who did Emma call?
  • Question tags: positive→negative tag, negative→positive tag; I'm..., aren't I?
  • So/Neither: "So am I" / "Neither do I"; I think so / I hope not
