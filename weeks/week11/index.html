Reported Speech 1 — Direct vs Reported Unit 45A

간접 화법 기본 — 직접 화법과 간접 화법 두 가지 표현 방법

📖 GIU Unit 45A, p.90

A Two ways to tell what somebody said: direct or reported speech

When you want to tell somebody what someone said, you can use either direct speech (인용부호로 원문 그대로) or reported speech (that-절로 간접).

📖 "There are two ways of doing this. You can repeat Dan's words (direct speech). Or you can use reported speech." (Unit 45A)

Direct speech: Dan said, "I am feeling sick." (인용부호 + 원문 그대로)
Reported speech: Dan said that he was feeling sick. (인용부호 X + 시제 후퇴)
Compare: direct Dan said, "I am feeling sick." vs reported Dan said that he was feeling sick.

Reported Speech 1 — Tense Backshift Unit 45B

간접 화법의 시제 후퇴 — 동사 형태 변환

📖 GIU Unit 45B, p.90

B The main verb is usually past — shift tense back

When reporting what someone said, the main verb is usually past, so the verb in the original direct speech also shifts back. You can leave out that.

📖 "In general, the present in direct speech changes to the past in reported speech." (Unit 45B)

Reported Speech 1 — past simple stays or → past perfect Unit 45C

간접화법에서 단순 과거는 그대로 두거나 과거완료로 바꿀 수 있음

C The simple past can stay the same OR change to the past perfect

📖 "The simple past (did/saw/knew, etc.) can stay the same in reported speech, or you can change it to the past perfect (had done / had seen / had known, etc.)." (Unit 45C)

Direct: "I woke up feeling sick, so I didn't go to work."
Reported (past simple — stays): Dan said (that) he woke up feeling sick, so he didn't go to work.
Reported (past perfect — also OK): Dan said (that) he had woken up feeling sick, so he hadn't gone to work.

📖 GIU Unit 45C, p.90

Reported Speech 2 — Don't always change verb Unit 46A

상황이 여전히 같으면 동사를 과거로 바꿀 필요 없음

📖 GIU Unit 46A, p.92

A If the situation is still the same, no need to change verb to past

We do not always change the verb in reported speech. If the situation is still the same, no need to shift back.

📖 "We do not always change the verb in reported speech. If the situation is still the same, it is not necessary to change the verb to the past." (Unit 46A)

Direct: John said, "My new job is boring." → Reported: John said that his new job is boring. (직장 여전히 boring → 시제 유지)
Direct: Olivia said, "I want to go to South America next year." → Reported: Olivia told me that she wants to go to South America next year. (여전히 가고 싶음)
But if situation changed/finished: John said that his new job was boring. (이제 더 이상 boring 아님 → 과거형)

Reported Speech 2 — When reality differs Unit 46B

말한 내용이 현재 사실과 다를 때는 반드시 과거형

📖 GIU Unit 46B, p.92

B Change verb when what was said differs from reality

You need to change the verb to past in reported speech when what was said is different from what is really true now.

📖 "You need to change the verb to the past in reported speech when what was said is different from what is really true." (Unit 46B)

Earlier (Lauren said): "Joe is in the hospital."
Later (you see Joe on the street): "Lauren said you were in the hospital." (NOT "you are in the hospital" — 더 이상 사실 아님)
핵심: Joe가 더 이상 병원에 없으므로 현재형으로 말할 수 없음. Reality와 다를 때 과거형 사용.

say vs. tell

tell / ask somebody to...

Questions 1 — Subject inversion & do/does/did Unit 47A-B

의문문 어순 기본 — subject 도치 + 단순 시제의 do/does/did

📖 GIU Unit 47A-B, p.94

A Subject comes AFTER the first verb (inversion)

In questions, subject comes after the first verb: subject + verb → verb + subject. 📖 Unit 47A

Will Tom be here tomorrow? / Have you been working hard? / When was the house built? / Is Katherine working today? (NOT "Is working Katherine")

B Use do/does/did for simple present/past — BUT not when who/what is subject

Simple present: do/does. Simple past: did. But: do/does/did 안 씀 if who/what is the subject.

Do you live nearby? / What time does the movie start? / Why did the train stop?
who = 목적어 (do/did 사용): "Emma called somebody." → Who did Emma call?
who = 주어 (do/did 없이): "Somebody called Emma." → Who called Emma? / What happened to you? (NOT "What did happen")

Questions 1 — Preposition at end & negative questions Unit 47C-D

전치사 끝 위치 + 부정 의문문 (놀라움/동의/제안)

📖 GIU Unit 47C-D, p.94

C Prepositions (in, for, etc.) usually go at the end of the question

의문사로 시작하는 의문문: 전치사는 문장 끝에 위치. 📖 Unit 47C

"Where are you from?" / "What was the weather like?" / "Who do you want to speak to?" / "Which job has Lauren applied for?"
격식체 (formal): "To whom do you wish to speak?" (preposition + whom)

D isn't it ...? / didn't you ...? — Negative questions (surprise, agreement, suggestions)

부정 의문문 — 놀라움 / 동의 기대 / "Why don't...?" 제안.

놀라움: "Didn't you hear the doorbell? I rang it three times." / "Haven't we met before?"
Yes/No 응답 주의: "Don't you want to go?" — "Yes." (=가고 싶다) / "No." (=가고 싶지 않다)
Why don't...? 제안: "Why don't we eat out tonight?" (NOT "Why we don't eat")

Questions 2 — Embedded questions (Do you know where...?) Unit 48A

내포 의문문 — statement 어순 + do/does/did 제거 + if/whether

📖 GIU Unit 48A, p.96

A Embedded questions: statement word order, drop do/does/did

의문문이 더 긴 문장의 일부일 때 (Do you know...? / I don't know... / Could you tell me...?), 어순이 평서문처럼 바뀌고 do/does/did 제거.

📖 "When the question (Where has Tom gone?) is part of a longer sentence (Do you know ...? / I don't know ... / Can you tell me ..., etc.), the word order changes." (Unit 48A)

Direct: "Where has Tom gone?" → Embedded: Do you know where Tom has gone? (NOT "has Tom gone")
Direct: "What time does the movie start?" → Embedded: Do you know what time the movie starts? (does 제거)
Direct: "Why did she leave early?" → Embedded: I wonder why she left early. (did 제거)
Yes/No 의문문 → if 또는 whether: "Did anybody see you?" → I don't know if anybody saw me. (or whether anybody saw me)

Questions 2 — Reported questions (He asked me where...) Unit 48B

간접 화법 의문문 — Unit 48A 어순 규칙 + 과거형 시제 (Unit 45 backshift)

📖 GIU Unit 48B, p.96

B Reported questions: same word order changes + past tense (backshift)

간접 화법 의문문도 Unit 48A의 어순 규칙 적용 + 본동사는 과거형으로 backshift (Unit 45 참조).

📖 "The same changes in word order happen in questions in reported speech." (Unit 48B)

Direct: "Where are you going?" → Reported: The police officer asked us where we were going.
Direct: "What time does the store close?" → Reported: Olivia wanted to know what time the store closed. (does 제거 + 과거형)
면접 예문: "Why did you apply?" → She asked why I had applied. / "How long have you been working?" → She asked how long I had been working.
Yes/No 의문문 → if/whether: "Are you willing to travel?" → She asked if (or whether) I was willing to travel.

Auxiliary Verbs — basics & interest reactions Unit 49A-B

보조동사: 반복 회피·부정 응답 / He has? — 놀라움·관심 표현

📖 GIU Unit 49A-B, p.98

A Auxiliary to avoid repeating + deny statements

Use an auxiliary (have/do/can/was/will, etc.) to avoid repeating the main verb, or to deny what somebody says. 📖 Unit 49A

"Have you locked the door?" "Yes, I have." / "Mike wasn't working, but Emily was." (= Emily was working)
"You'll be the next president." "No, I won't." (부정 응답)

B He has? / She isn't? / You are? — show interest or surprise

Use He has? / She isn't? / You are?, etc. to show interest or surprise. 📖 Unit 49B

"Josh has changed a lot." — "He has? I haven't seen him lately."
"I'm getting married." — "You are? Really?" (놀라움)

so do I / neither do I & I think so Unit 49C-D

so/neither (동의 표현, 도치) + I think so (절 반복 회피)

📖 GIU Unit 49C-D, p.98

C so and neither — agreement (verb before subject)

so + auxiliary + subject (긍정 동의) / neither + auxiliary + subject (부정 동의). 📖 Unit 49C (verb before subject)

"I'm tired." — "So am I." (= I'm tired too) / "I never read newspapers." — "Neither do I."
"I passed the exam, and so did Paul." (NOT "so Paul did")

D I think so / I hope so / I suppose so — avoid repeating clause

부정 형태: I don't think so | I hope not | I'm afraid not | I guess not | I suppose not

"Are those people Korean?" — "I think so." (= I think they are Korean)
"Do you think it will rain?" — "I hope not." (NOT "I don't hope so")

Tag Questions — Basics & affirmative/negative rules Unit 50A-B

부가 의문문 — 기본 사용 + 긍정/부정 태그 규칙 + Yes/No 응답 의미

📖 GIU Unit 50A-B, p.100

A Tag questions = mini-questions at the end of a sentence

Have you? / Wasn't it? 등이 tag questions. 보조동사 사용 (have/was/will, etc.). 단순 현재/과거엔 do/does/did. 📖 Unit 50A

"Emily plays the piano, doesn't she?" / "You didn't lock the door, did you?" "No, I forgot."

B Affirmative + negative tag / Negative + affirmative tag

긍정문 + 부정 태그 (보통) / 부정문 + 긍정 태그

긍정 + 부정: "Kate will be here soon, won't she?" / "There was a lot of traffic, wasn't there?"
부정 + 긍정: "Kate won't be late, will she?" / "You haven't eaten yet, have you?"
⚠️ Yes/No 응답 의미: "You're not going out, are you?" — "Yes." (=나갈 거다) / "No." (=안 나갈 거다)

Tag Questions — Intonation meaning & special tags Unit 50C-D

억양에 따른 의미 + 특수 태그 형태

📖 GIU Unit 50C-D, p.100

C Meaning depends on intonation — voice down (agreement) or up (real question)

의미는 억양에 따라: 목소리 ↓ = 동의 기대 (질문 아님), 목소리 ↑ = 진짜 질문. 📖 Unit 50C

↓ (동의 기대): "It's a nice day, isn't it ↘?" "Yes, beautiful." / "David doesn't look well, does he ↘?"
↑ (진짜 질문): "You haven't seen Kate today, have you ↗?" (= Have you seen Kate today?)
부정+긍정 태그 ↑ = 부탁: "You couldn't do me a favor, could you ↗?"

D Special tag forms: imperative → will you? / I'm → aren't I?

명령문 뒤: "Listen to me, will you?" / "I'm..." 뒤: "I'm right, aren't I?" (NOT "am I not?")

참고 (Supplementary) — 교재 본문 외

"Let's..." 뒤: "Let's go, shall we?" / 부정어(nobody/nothing) → 긍정 태그: "Nobody called, did they?"

Direct vs. Reported Speech Units 45–46

직접 화법 vs. 간접 화법

Direct Speech

Dan said, "I'm feeling sick."

Anna said, "I'll call you when I get back."

Uses quotation marks, exact words, original tenses

Reported Speech

Dan said he was feeling sick.

Anna said she would call me when she got back.

No quotation marks, tense backshift, pronoun changes

📖 GIU Units 45–46

Common Mistakes for Korean Speakers

한국어 화자의 흔한 실수

L1 Interference: No Tense Backshift
"He said he is tired." → "He said he was tired."
Backshift: "is" → "was" 시제 후퇴: "is" → "was"
L1 Interference: Question Word Order
"He asked where did I live." → "He asked where I lived."
No inversion, no "did" — statement word order. 도치/did 없이 평서문 어순.

Week 11 Summary

11주차 요약

  • Tense backshift: present→past, past→past perfect, will→would, can→could
  • Say vs. tell: tell + somebody; say (no person); tell/ask somebody to...
  • Reported questions: statement word order, no "do/did"; embedded: Do you know where...?
  • Who/what subject: Who called Emma? (no did) vs. Who did Emma call?
  • Question tags: positive→negative tag, negative→positive tag; I'm..., aren't I?
  • So/Neither: "So am I" / "Neither do I"; I think so / I hope not
