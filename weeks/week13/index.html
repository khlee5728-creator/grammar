prefer and would prefer Unit 57A-B

prefer + 동명사 vs 부정사 (의미 차이 없음) / would prefer는 to부정사만

A prefer to do / prefer doing

prefer + -ing  /  prefer + to do — general preference (no difference in meaning)

▸ prefer A to B (with -ing on both)

"I prefer driving to taking the train."

▸ prefer to do A rather than (do) B

"I prefer to drive rather than take the train."

B would prefer (I'd prefer ...) — specific situation

▸ would prefer + noun

"Would you prefer coffee or tea?"

▸ would prefer + to do (not usually would prefer doing)

"I'd prefer to drive."

would rather + base form Unit 57C-D

would rather + 동사원형 (to 없음) / I'd rather sb did (가정법 = base form)

C would rather + base form (I'd rather ...)

Subject + would rather + base form — NOT to do
"I'd rather drive." (not to drive)
"I'd rather stay home than go to the movies."

▸ Negative: I'd rather not ...

"I'm tired. I'd rather not go out tonight."
A: "Do you want to go out?" B: "I'd rather not."

D I'd rather somebody do something — subjunctive (= base form, also past form)

"I'd rather you drive." (base form / subjunctive)
"I'd rather he do it today." (base form / subjunctive)
"...would you rather I tell her?" (base form / subjunctive)

(verb / adjective / noun) + preposition + -ing

Preposition + -ing — before / after / by / without Unit 58

before / after / by / without + 동명사

B before/after/by/without + -ing

"Before to go out, I called Sarah."
"Before going out, I called Sarah." — before + -ing (preposition + -ing, NOT to + verb)

▸ Other prepositions: by (method) / without (negative manner)

"You can improve your English by reading more." — by + -ing (method / means)

to + -ing (look forward to doing, etc.) Unit 58C

"to"가 부정사 marker일 때 vs 전치사일 때 (= + -ing)

C "to" has two roles — infinitive marker OR preposition

① to + base form (infinitive)  |  ② to + noun / -ing (preposition) 📖 Unit 58C

When "to" is a preposition (look forward to / prefer ... to / be used to / admit to), the verb takes -ing (NOT base form)

▸ "to" + noun — preposition role made clear

"Are you looking forward to the weekend?" — to + noun

▸ "to" + -ing — same preposition, verb becomes -ing

"Are you looking forward to go on vacation?"
"Are you looking forward to going on vacation?"
"I prefer driving to taking the train." (not to take)
"They admitted to stealing the money." (not to steal)

📖 GIU Unit 58C, p.116

be/get used to ... (I'm used to ...) Unit 59A

Rachel 예시 (be/get used to driving on the left)

A Study this example situation — Rachel

Rachel is American, but she lives in Tokyo. When she first drove a car in Tokyo, driving on the left was a problem (Americans drive on the right). 📖 Unit 59A

▸ Timeline — becoming accustomed (process)

At first: "She wasn't used to it." / "She wasn't used to driving on the left."
After a while: "She got used to driving on the left."
Now: "She is used to driving on the left."

📖 GIU Unit 59A, p.118

I'm used to something — rule + "to" as preposition Unit 59B-C

be/get used to 공식 + 다양한 예문 / "to" = 전치사

B I'm used to something = it is not new or strange for me

Subject + be / get used to + something / doing something
"John lives alone ... He's used to it. He is used to living alone."
"I bought new shoes. They felt strange because I wasn't used to them."
"We'll get used to the noise, but right now it's very disturbing."
"Katherine's husband is often away. She's used to him being away."

C "to" is a preposition — so "be/get used to doing" (NOT to do)

When we say "I am used to ...", to is a preposition. 📖 Unit 59C

"Rachel is used to driving" (NOT used to drive)  |  "I'm used to living alone" (NOT used to live)

📖 GIU Unit 59B-C, p.118

Compare: I am used to doing vs I used to do Unit 59D

be used to (익숙) vs used to (과거 습관) 의미 비교

D I am used to doing vs I used to do — different meanings

I am used to (doing) something = it isn't unusual or new for me (accustomed now)

I used to do something = past habit (no longer true)

📖 Unit 59D

▸ I am used to + -ing/noun — accustomed (familiar now)

"I'm used to the weather here."
"I'm used to driving on the left because I've lived in Japan for a long time."

▸ I used to + base form — past habit (no longer true)

"I used to drive to work every day, but these days I usually ride my bike."
"We used to live just outside the city, but now we live near downtown."

Common Mistakes for Korean Speakers

한국어 화자가 자주 하는 실수

⚠ L1 Interference: "would rather" + "to"

Korean speakers often add "to" after "would rather" because "to" is common in other patterns.

"I'd rather to stay home."
"I'd rather stay home." — would rather + base form (no to)
⚠ L1 Interference: Confusing "used to do" and "be used to doing"

Korean has one expression for both meanings, making this distinction difficult.

"I'm used to get up early."
"I'm used to getting up early." — "be used to" + -ing (not base form)

Week 13 Summary

  • prefer: + -ing or + to + verb (both OK); prefer A to B / prefer to do rather than do
  • would rather: + base form (NEVER "to"); I'd rather somebody did
  • Preposition + -ing: without stopping, by reading, in spite of feeling
  • "to" as preposition: look forward to doing, prefer ... to doing
  • used to + base: past habit / be/get used to + -ing: accustomed to
  • Verb + prep + -ing: feel like doing, insist on doing, succeed in doing
  • Expressions + -ing: it's worth doing, have trouble doing, spend time doing
